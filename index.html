About me

Hi! I'm a student currently studying at Université Paris-Saclay, finishing my bachelor in computer science.
I was born and raised in Bucharest and I moved to France at 18 for my undergraduate studies. I am pursuing my third year of bachelor's, Licence Double-Diplôme Informatique, Mathématiques within the Magistère Informatique program.
I am particularly interested in theoretical computer science, especially algorithmics and graph theory. I have pursued these interests through two internships at LISN, within the GALAC team: a two-month internship focused on a codind project involving graph coloring, and a nine-month internship on graph reconstruction.
My native language is romanian but I also have a C2 level in english and a C1 level in french.

Research internships

  1. December 2025 - August 2026

    Study of graph reconstruction

    LISN, Université Paris-Saclay, supervised by Hoang La and Johanne Cohen

    During this nine-month research internship I worked on graph reconstruction problems. In doing so, I had the chance to discover this subject and at the same time learn about Ramsey theory, graph algorithms, and randomized algorithms. Moreover, it was a great opportunity to see what research looks like: how an open problem is approached, how a literature review is carried out, and how an article is written. During this internship I contributed to the main result, an algorithm for reconstructing graphs, and helped write the article in LaTeX.

  2. May - June 2025

    Coding project of a graph coloring game

    LISN, Université Paris-Saclay, supervised by Hoang La and Clément Chenevière

    During this internship I developed a web game in which the user is given a graph to color, with the aim of introducing them to some graph theory along the way. The project allowed me to strengthen my Python skills. I also dicovered how to use SageMath, and about its contribution process. The game's interface was built in JavaScript, HTML and CSS using a web framework, and the project as a whole was an opportinity to learn proper Git practices.

Workshops

  1. July 2026

    Workshop on a word reconstruction problem

    LISN, Université Paris-Saclay

    The three-day workshop focused on a word reconstruction problem. We studied which partitions of a word's k-subsequences allow the original word to be uniquely reconstructed. During this experience, I learned about different proof methods, how to approach an open problem, and what collaborative work in computer science research looks like. I had the chance to exchange ideas and work with people with different perspectives and expertise in graph theory.
